Case library / 08 — Real-time 3D

A mechanical octopus you can take apart.

Benthos-8 is an original jet-driven octopod built entirely from code: open its head, its mantle and its arms, and every part, gauge and chart moves from the same model.

01

The brief

Exploded diagrams and cutaways are how engineers explain machines, but on paper they are frozen. The brief was to make one that moves: a machine you can open part by part while it keeps running, with the instruments beside it telling the truth about what it is doing.

The subject is an invented survey octopod that swims by jet, like the real animal: the mantle squeezes water out through a siphon. That gives one input that drives the whole machine, and a body with plenty to open: a head, a mantle and eight long arms.

02

Design decisions

01

One jet model drives everything. Mantle volume, stroke and nozzle area give flow, jet speed, thrust and pressure. The mantle breathes with that model, the pump and drive motor turn at the speeds it sets, and every chart, the 2D section, the callouts and a live hand check of the thrust formula read from it.

02

Every gear turns at its real ratio. The drive motor feeds a 4:1 planetary, whose carrier turns a 48-tooth crown gear meshing with eight 12-tooth bevel pinions, one per arm, each winding a tendon spool through a clutch. A 22-to-32-tooth timing belt takes the motor up to the jet pump.

03

The page runs itself and then hands over. A cursor walks the console and presses the buttons; any click, drag or key gives control to the visitor, and 25 seconds of quiet gives it back. Two sliders, water current and nozzle diameter, feed straight into the model, so the machine can be pushed until it drifts or overpressures.

03

Outcome

A machine people can take apart in the browser: the head swings open on its drivetrain, the mantle's upper panels hinge open on actuators over the pump and cell packs, arm R2 comes off its hub, and the whole thing explodes on a turntable and reassembles. It opens in jet x-ray, so the inside shows from the first frame.
